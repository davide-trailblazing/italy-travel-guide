Italy, through the eyes of a local

No tourist traps, no menu photos, no cappuccino after lunch. Just the plates, the corners and the swims I'd send a friend to — eight places, the way we actually live them.

The classics North Sicily

— from an Italian, to you

For every place, four things the way a local sees them: what to eat (and where not to), what to see, where to swim, and a 🤜 local tip — the little move we keep for ourselves.

The classic triangle

Rome · Florence · Venice

History, art and the four-pasta dinner — but eaten where the streets get narrow, not on the piazza with the guy waving a menu.

Rome

👋 Eat: Cacio e pepe

🍝 What to eat

Stick to the four Roman pastas and you can't go wrong: cacio e pepe, gricia, amatriciana, carbonara (no cream, ever, it's just egg, guanciale and pecorino). For dinner we eat where the streets get narrow in Testaccio or further out in the Quadraro, never on a piazza with a guy waving a menu at you. Order a supplì while you wait and finish with carciofo alla romana when the artichokes are in season, late winter into spring.

🏛 What to see

Rome runs on rhythm: long lunch, the city goes quiet in the early afternoon, then everything wakes up for the passeggiata and an aperitivo around seven. We don't rush coffee, we drink it standing at the bar (you pay less) and never order a cappuccino after a meal, that's a dead giveaway. Go to the big sights at opening or at golden hour and spend the heat of the day where the locals are, in the alleys of Trastevere or Monti.

🏖 Where to swim

Rome isn't a beach city, but in summer we escape to the sea at Sperlonga or the cleaner stretches south, and Romans pile onto the trains to Anzio and Santa Marinella. For a proper swim skip Ostia's crowded free beach and go a bit further down the coast where the water's clearer; bring cash for a sunbed at a stabilimento.

🤜 Local tip

Every old fountain with a little spout, the nasoni, runs free cold drinking water; cap the bottom hole with your thumb and it arcs up like a fountain to drink, so you never pay for water in summer. And go to the Trevi or the Pantheon at 7am, you'll have them nearly to yourself before the tour buses land.

Florence

👋 Eat: Bistecca alla fiorentina

🍝 What to eat

We eat the bistecca rare and thick, never well-done, and never on the tourist piazze where they charge you for the view. Come to my side of the river, the Oltrarno around Santo Spirito, where the streets get narrow and the trattorie still write the menu by hand: trippa or lampredotto from a street cart for lunch, peposo or ribollita when it gets cold. Order the house Chianti in the carafe, sfuso, and you'll thank me.

🏛 What to see

Florence is small, so we walk it on foot and we go early, before the cruise crowds choke the Ponte Vecchio. Skip the two-hour Uffizi line in August and book a slot, then come hang in Santo Spirito at aperitivo when the whole quartiere spills onto the steps of the church. The real city is the artisan botteghe in the Oltrarno, the gilders and leather guys still working like it's 1500.

🏖 Where to swim

Florence has no sea, so on a hot day we drive an hour into the Mugello or the Casentino to swim in the river pools, cold and clean under the trees. If you want the proper Tuscan coast, the Maremma south of Grosseto is where we go, wild beaches and pine forest, not the umbrella-row resorts.

🤜 Local tip

Go up to Piazzale Michelangelo for the view like everyone says, but then keep climbing five more minutes to San Miniato al Monte, where the monks sing Gregorian chant at vespers around sunset and almost no tourist makes it that far. That's the Florence we keep for ourselves.

Venice

👋 Eat: Cicchetti and a spritz

🍝 What to eat

Forget the sit-down menus near San Marco with the photos outside, that is for the tour buses. We eat cicchetti standing at a bacaro: little bread with baccalà mantecato (whipped salt cod), sarde in saor (sweet-sour sardines with onions), a fried meatball, washed down with an ombra of wine. Do it the proper way, hop two or three bacari in a row before dinner, around 6pm, that is our aperitivo.

🏛 What to see

Venice is not a museum, real people still live here, so we walk fast, keep right, and never stop on a bridge to take a photo. Go early, the city before 9am is ours again, the light on the canals is unreal and you hear footsteps instead of suitcases. Skip the gondola circus at the main stops; if you want the water, take the traghetto, the standing gondola that ferries locals across the Grand Canal for a couple of euros.

🏖 Where to swim

The proper swim is the Lido, our long sandy beach island, a short vaporetto ride away, best in late spring or September when the crowds thin out. For something wilder we go further to the lagoon islands or the beaches toward Cavallino; clean Adriatic water, real sand, no postcard hustle.

🤜 Local tip

Buy your fish and produce at the Rialto market in the morning, around 8 to 9, then disappear into Cannaregio or Castello for lunch, where the alleys get narrow and the prices get honest. The rule: the closer to Piazza San Marco, the worse and pricier the food, so always walk away from the crowd, not toward it.

The North — coast & lakes

Cinque Terre · Lake Garda

Liguria's vertical villages and the alpine-clear lakes. Pesto pounded in a mortar, lake fish, and a swim off the rocks.

Cinque Terre

👋 Eat: Trofie al pesto

🍝 What to eat

Pesto is ours, so eat it where they still pound it in a marble mortar: trofie or trenette al pesto, with a green bean and a slice of potato thrown in the pot, that's how nonna does it. Get a cono of fried anchovies and calamari from a friggitoria and walk with it, skip the sit-down "seafood platters" near the station. And the focaccia, plain or with onion in the morning, dipped in your cappuccino like we do, nobody's judging.

🏛 What to see

We live vertically here, on terraces our grandfathers cut into the cliff and held up with dry-stone walls, the muretti a secco, so respect that this is a working village, not a postcard. Mornings are slow and the riposo in the afternoon is real, shutters down, so plan around it. Take the sentiero, the old footpaths between villages, instead of only the train, and you'll see the vineyards and the capers growing wild on the walls.

🏖 Where to swim

The swimming is rock and pebble, not sand, so bring water shoes, scogli are slippery. Monterosso has the only real beach with loungers, but locals slip down to the little coves, like the cala past the Vernazza harbour, and jump straight off the rocks into deep clear water.

🤜 Local tip

Come in early June or September and start your day at 7am: the trains and trails are empty, the light on the water is best, and you'll have the villages to yourself before the day-trippers arrive around 10. Buy the Cinque Terre Card only if you're hiking the paid trails, otherwise just get point-to-point train tickets, it's cheaper. And buy a bottle of Sciacchetrà, our sweet passito wine, straight from a small grower, not the souvenir shops.

Lake Garda

👋 Eat: Lavarello al lago

🍝 What to eat

We eat the lake: lavarello (whitefish) grilled or breaded, sardine essiccate (sun-dried, marinated lake sardines) the old ladies still make, and a plate of bigoli pasta. With it, always our own oil, the Garda DOP that's lighter and more delicate than the southern stuff, and a glass of Bardolino or a Lugana white from the south shore.

🏛 What to see

Garda is three regions in one lake, so a Veronese on the east shore and a Brescian on the west bicker over everything but agree on the spritz at 6. We do the passeggiata along the lungolago at dusk, then sit. Skip the loud lakefront terraces packed with tour groups; walk two streets back into the centro storico where the alleys narrow and the prices halve.

🏖 Where to swim

The lake is clean and swimmable, coldest and clearest up north toward Riva and Torbole where the water turns alpine and the windsurfers live. For lazy swimming we go to the pebbly coves around the Sirmione peninsula or the Baia delle Sirene near Garda town, early morning before the day-trippers claim the rocks.

🤜 Local tip

Take the slow battello (the public ferry, not a tour boat) across the lake instead of driving the clogged shore road, get off at a small port like Punta San Vigilio at golden hour, and you'll have one of the prettiest corners of Garda almost to yourself. Come May or September, never August.

Sicily

Ortigia · Taormina · Lipari

Greek temples, volcanic water and ricotta in everything sweet. Start the day with a granita and a brioche.

Ortigia

👋 Eat: Pasta con le sarde

🍝 What to eat

Pasta con le sarde is the one, sardines, wild fennel, pine nuts, that sweet-savoury thing we do here, and a plate of polpo (octopus) bought fresh and dressed simply. For street food we grab a cartoccio of fried fish or, in the morning, a focaccia stuffed with stuff from the bakery. Skip the trattorie ringed around Piazza Duomo with the photo menus and the guy waving you in; walk two minutes until the vicoli (little lanes) get narrow and you'll find where we actually eat.

🏛 What to see

Ortigia is the old island, the heart of Siracusa, layered Greek-on-Norman-on-Baroque, and the Duomo is literally a Greek temple with a church grown around it, you can still see the ancient columns in the walls. We live by the passeggiata: late afternoon into evening everyone strolls, you sit for an aperitivo, you greet half the street. Go slow and look up, the best things are the stone, the light at golden hour, and the sea at the end of every alley.

🏖 Where to swim

We swim right off the rocks, the spots along the Lungomare and around the Forte Vigliena where locals lay their towels on the stone and dive into clear, deep water, no sand needed. Cala Rossa and the little ladders into the sea are ours; if you want a proper beach you go out to Fontane Bianche or Arenella, but for a quick dip the island itself is perfect, June to September the water is warm and blue.

🤜 Local tip

Go to the Mercato di Ortigia early, before 1pm, that's when it's alive, fishmongers shouting, tomatoes, capers, ricotta, and the famous panino spot where they slice you mortadella, cheese and sun-dried tomatoes to order, then eat it standing on the steps by the sea. Don't bother after lunch, the market closes and the magic is gone; mornings are the secret, and skip the overpriced gelato on the square, the good one is tucked back in the lanes.

Taormina

👋 Eat: Granita con brioche

🍝 What to eat

Breakfast is granita di mandorla (almond) or gelsi (mulberry) with a brioche col tuppo to dunk, the way we actually start a summer morning. For real food skip the menus with photos on Corso Umberto and the square; walk until the streets get narrow and steep, where the little places do pasta alla Norma, fresh sarde, and arancini that aren't reheated. Save room for a cannolo filled to order, never the ones sitting pre-filled in a tray.

🏛 What to see

Do Corso Umberto early or at passeggiata hour around 7pm when the light goes gold and the day-trippers have left for their cruise ships. The Greek Theatre is worth it with Etna smoking behind the stage, but go right at opening or for a summer concert, not midday in the crush. We treat the evening as the main event, not the noon heat.

🏖 Where to swim

Taormina town sits on a cliff, so the swimming is down at Isola Bella and Mazzarò, reached by the funivia (cable car) rather than the brutal walk back up. Isola Bella is a pebble cove, stunning but packed by 11am, so go early or take a boat around to the little grottos and swim off the back of Capo Sant'Andrea where it's quieter and clear.

🤜 Local tip

Don't sleep in Taormina if you want the real side: base in Castelmola just above (10 minutes up) for a wine-almond at the old bar and a view over the whole coast, or come down to Catania's fish market, A Piscaria, on a weekday morning, then drive up Etna in the afternoon to eat at a cantina among the lava vineyards. That loop, sea in the morning, volcano by dusk, is what we'd actually do with a friend.

Lipari

👋 Eat: Pane cunzato

🍝 What to eat

We don't sit down for a heavy lunch in summer; we eat pane cunzato, a fat slab of bread dressed with our tomatoes, capers, oregano, anchovy and a flood of olive oil, standing up between swims. For dinner go where the streets get narrow behind the corso, not the places with photo menus on Marina Corta, and order whatever pasta they push that day with capers and swordfish. Finish with a glass of cold Malvasia, our sweet wine, and granita di gelsi if the mulberries are in.

🏛 What to see

Lipari runs on the passeggiata: at golden hour everyone walks the corso up to the castello, the old fortress on the rock where the cathedral and the small archaeological museum sit. The island empties of day-trippers around 5pm when the hydrofoils leave, and that's when it becomes ours again, so plan your evening for after that. We talk loud, we're not rushing, and August is the festa di San Bartolomeo, our patron, with fireworks over the harbor.

🏖 Where to swim

Skip pebbly Canneto's main strip and walk north to Spiaggia Bianca, the old pumice beach where the water glows turquoise from the white seabed. For the cleanest swim take a boat to the Cave di Pomice or around to the faraglioni rocks; the water there is deep and impossibly blue, best from morning to early afternoon before the wind picks up.

🤜 Local tip

Rent a little gozzo (wooden boat) for half a day from a fisherman at Marina Corta rather than booking a packaged tour, and go early to swim under the cliffs before the tour groups arrive. And don't burn your whole trip on Lipari town, hop the 10-minute ferry to Vulcano for the sulphur and the black sand, or to tiny Salina for the best granita and the real Malvasia.
