Italy travel guide

Six places worth the trip, each with what to eat, what to see, and where to swim. From the classic cities of the mainland to the sea and food of Sicily.

Mainland Sicily

For every place you will find three things: a food recommendation, the culture not to miss, and the beaches (spiagge) nearby. Each area is also tagged with its signature food.

Mainland

Rome · Florence · Venice

The classic Italian triangle: history, art, and food, with the coast never too far away.

Rome

Signature food: carbonara

🍝 Food

Roman pasta classics: carbonara, cacio e pepe, and amatriciana. Try suppli (fried rice balls) and pizza al taglio. The Testaccio and Trastevere areas are full of good trattorias.

🏛 Culture

The Colosseum, the Roman Forum, and the Pantheon, plus the Vatican Museums and St. Peter's Basilica. Toss a coin in the Trevi Fountain.

🏖 Beaches (spiagge)

Rome is inland but the sea is close. Ostia is about 30 minutes by train; Fregene and the white sand of Sabaudia (about 1 hour) are popular day trips.

Florence

Signature food: bistecca alla fiorentina

🍝 Food

The bistecca alla fiorentina is a thick T-bone steak cooked rare. Also try ribollita (bread and vegetable soup), a lampredotto sandwich, and Chianti wine from the Tuscan hills.

🏛 Culture

The home of the Renaissance: the Duomo with Brunelleschi's dome, the Uffizi Gallery, Michelangelo's David at the Accademia, and the Ponte Vecchio.

🏖 Beaches (spiagge)

Florence is inland with no beach of its own. The nearest coast is Versilia (Viareggio and Forte dei Marmi), about 1.5 hours by car or train.

Venice

Signature food: cicchetti

🍝 Food

Cicchetti are small Venetian bar snacks, great with a glass of wine or a spritz. Try sarde in saor (sweet and sour sardines) and risotto al nero di seppia (cuttlefish ink).

🏛 Culture

St. Mark's Basilica and Square, the Doge's Palace, the Grand Canal, and the Rialto Bridge. Take a vaporetto to the islands of Murano and Burano.

🏖 Beaches (spiagge)

The Lido di Venezia is the city's own beach island, a short boat ride away. Jesolo and Cavallino-Treporti nearby have long sandy beaches.

Sicily

Ortigia · Taormina · Lipari

The signature food of Sicily is ricotta: it fills cannoli and cassata and shows up in pastries across the island. Pair it with a granita and a brioche for breakfast.

Ortigia

Signature food: ricotta

🍝 Food

Sicilian sweets built on ricotta (cannoli and cassata), plus fresh seafood, arancini, and granita with brioche. The Ortigia street market is a highlight.

🏛 Culture

The old-town island of Siracusa: a Duomo built inside an ancient Greek temple, the Fountain of Arethusa, and Baroque streets. Nearby is the Neapolis park with its Greek theatre.

🏖 Beaches (spiagge)

Small swimming spots like Cala Rossa on Ortigia itself, then Fontane Bianche and Arenella a short drive south, and the Vendicari reserve (Calamosche) further on.

Taormina

Signature food: ricotta

🍝 Food

Ricotta sweets and almond treats, granita, and pasta alla Norma (with fried aubergine and salted ricotta). Many terraces look out over Mount Etna.

🏛 Culture

The ancient Greek-Roman Theatre, framed by Etna and the sea, is the star. Stroll Corso Umberto and the Villa Comunale gardens in the medieval old town.

🏖 Beaches (spiagge)

Isola Bella is a tiny island and nature reserve below the town; Mazzaro is reachable by cable car. Giardini Naxos and Letojanni have wider beaches.

Lipari

Signature food: ricotta

🍝 Food

Aeolian seafood, capers, and Sicilian ricotta sweets and granita. Try the sweet Malvasia wine made on the islands.

🏛 Culture

The largest of the Aeolian Islands, a UNESCO-listed volcanic group. Visit the Lipari castle and its archaeological museum, then take a boat to Vulcano and Stromboli.

🏖 Beaches (spiagge)

Canneto, the white pumice of Spiaggia Bianca, Valle Muria, and Acquacalda. The water is clear and the scenery is volcanic.
